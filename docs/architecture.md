# Architecture

The app is a SwiftUI shell with an About sheet and the accepted sound bank. The local `LowlightPlanning` package owns native song plans, note events, a shared transition clock, and intent checkpoints. `LowlightAudio` prepares guitar/piano/bass/percussion samples off the audio thread and renders them through its small C++ `CLowlightDSP` target; see the [tonal preparation](validation-tonal.md), [tonal voice](validation-voices.md), [bass](validation-bass.md), [percussion](validation-percussion.md), [rhythm bus](validation-rhythm.md), [linked harmonic compression](validation-glue.md), [stereo room](validation-room.md), [linked peak protection](validation-peak.md), [song rack fades](validation-fades.md), [quiet air](validation-air.md), and [song mix](validation-mix.md) evidence. The optional bus view applies configured song fades before filters/glue/room and retains separate guitar/piano, processed bass/drums, and kick duck control for the harmonic rack; both output views share one voice pool and continuous state. A separate fixed-storage processor applies harmonic focus and linked compression, delaying all bus fields by 399 frames and explicitly draining the finite endpoint. A causal fixed-storage stereo room follows compression, preserves rhythm alignment, and keeps its tail alive through continued zero input. A separate linked peak protector supports song/session ceilings, adds 160 frames of lookahead, and explicitly drains at end of input without requiring a known stream length. A finite quiet-air stage prepares whole-channel normalization off-thread, then replays compatible noise into the mix with fixed storage and shared absolute tonal fades. A song mix stage combines the aligned room/rhythm buses with ducking and prepared air, then applies HP32 before fixed loudness gain. A separate [body loudness meter](validation-loudness.md) consumes that stereo body during preparation with fixed storage and freshly initialized K-weighting; a [prepared fixed gain](validation-gain.md) then scales every song frame before song peak protection. Selecting the body and orchestrating preparation/replay remain future work. There is no playback engine, audio session, settings persistence, or external package dependency; the small adapted random-generation subset retains its [upstream notices](third-party-code.md). Resource membership is explicit in the Xcode project and package manifest. Reference score imports preserve [original note lengths](validation-reference-timing.md) while native plans retain their shared absolute endpoint clock.

Responsibilities stay separate: deterministic Swift planner and event timeline, bank preparation, a small C/C++ DSP kernel, playback coordinator, persistence, and UI. Prefer a few modules with clear ownership. See the [native planning decision](adrs/002-native-planning.md) for algorithm versioning and bounded history, and the [shared-time decision](adrs/003-events-and-shared-time.md) for transitions and checkpoint limits.

Score preparation fully processes and validates each unique note, then retains only the tonal/bass prefix reachable by its longest event. Percussion keeps its complete shaped body. The fixed sample/event/PCM limits and explicit capacity failures remain in force; see [sample storage validation](validation-sample-storage.md).

The audio callback will render from preallocated storage only. No planning, decoding, allocation, locks, logging, I/O, async calls, or UI updates belong there. Plan one successor, use at most two song racks, and enforce 64 total voices with deterministic stealing. Handle arbitrary callback frame counts in bounded subblocks.

Preserve the canonical 32 kHz signal path and convert at the output boundary. Keep effect and limiter state continuous. Offline rendering must exercise production DSP. Whole-song gain analysis needs a bounded-memory pass off the callback; its startup and CPU costs must be measured.

Persist versioned musical intent atomically off the callback. Restoring a checkpoint does not restore DSP state. Relaunch must stay silent until Play, then restore gracefully with a fade.
